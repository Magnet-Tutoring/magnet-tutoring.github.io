Grade Calculator

Free tools to help you understand exactly where you stand in a class: your current grade, what you need on the final, the highest grade you can still reach, and the lowest score that holds your grade. Everything stays in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

1. What's my current grade?

Enter each category (like Tests, Homework, Quizzes), the weight it's worth in your class, and your average score in it. You can use up to 10 categories. Leave a row blank and it counts as 0% (ignored). We'll compute your overall grade.

2. What do I need on the final?

Enter your current grade, the grade you're hoping to finish with, and how much the final (or remaining work) is worth. We'll tell you the score you need.

For example, if the final is 20% of your grade, enter 20.

3. What's the highest grade I can still get?

If you aced everything left in the class, how high could you finish? Enter your current grade and how much of the grade is still unscored (the final plus any remaining assignments).

For example, if the final is 20% and a project is 10%, enter 30.

4. What's the lowest I can score and still hold my grade?

Want to know how much cushion you have? Enter your current grade, the grade you need to keep (like 90 for an A-), and the weight of your remaining work. We'll find the lowest score on that work that still keeps you at or above your target.

The math behind the maximum (for the curious)

Your course grade is a weighted average. If your current grade is C (as a percent) and the remaining work is worth a fraction w of the grade, then your finish is:

Final grade = C·(1 − w) + (your remaining score)·w

Your highest possible finish comes from scoring 100% on everything left. You can see this with a quick bit of calculus: treat the final grade as a function of your remaining score and take its derivative. The slope with respect to that score is just w, which is positive. A function whose slope is always positive is strictly increasing, so (by the Extreme Value Theorem) its maximum on the allowed range sits at the top boundary, a perfect 100%. Plugging that in gives the clean formula the "ceiling" tool uses:

Maximum grade = C·(1 − w) + 100·w

So the calculus confirms the common-sense answer: the best you can do is lock in what you've earned and ace what's left. No hidden "effort" or "efficiency" fudge factors, just the exact math your gradebook uses. (If a formula promised to predict your actual max based on you personally, it would be guessing, and real improvement comes from the work, which is where a tutor helps.)
